Before you begin · Once per board

Turn on the side toolbar

The board's tools live in a side toolbar that opens from the edges of the screen, so you can reach them without standing in front of your students. If you don't see small arrows on the left and right edges of your board, turn the toolbar on once:

  1. Open Settings on the board.
  2. Go to Display and scroll to the bottom.
  3. Turn on the sliding menu (side toolbar) option.
  4. Two arrows appear on the left and right edges. Tap either one to open the toolbar.

Display content on your classroom display

Three ways to get your content on the board. Pick the one that fits today's lesson.

01
Not sure which to use? Where is today's lesson?

Option 1 · No device needed

The board's built-in Chrome browser

Sign in once and everything in your Google Drive is on the board, along with any website you want to show.

  1. Wake the board and open Chrome from the home screen.
  2. Sign in with your FPS Google account.
  3. Open anything from Drive, Slides, Docs or Classroom, or go to any website.

If you share this board with another teacher, sign out of Chrome when you're done so they don't have access to your Drive and email.

Option 2 · Wired

USB-C cable from your Chromebook

A wired connection that mirrors or extends your Chromebook, the same way you connected your Windows PC before.

  1. Connect your Chromebook to the board with a USB-C to USB-C cable.
  2. If your screen doesn't appear, switch the board to that USB-C input.
  3. To choose between mirror and extend, on the Chromebook go to Settings → Device → Displays.

SMART Boards need a USB-C to HDMI adapter. Ask us if your room needs one.

Option 3 · Wireless · Not guaranteed

The board's built-in mirroring app

Mirror or extend your screen without a cable, so you can teach from anywhere in the room. Everything on your screen, including notifications, shows on the board.

Before you rely on this Wireless mirroring depends on our network allowing it, and support varies by board model. Test it in your own classroom before you plan a lesson around it, and have Option 1 or 2 ready.
  1. On the board, open its mirroring app (pick your board below).
  2. Note the display name and any code it shows.
  3. On your device, connect to that display name and follow the instructions the board shows.
  4. Enter the code or PIN if the board asks for one.
Keep the PIN private Don't share the board's code or PIN with students, and keep it off anything they can see. Some boards, including the Wacebo boards, broadcast their own Wi-Fi network. Anyone with the PIN could join that network or put their own screen on your board without your permission.
Your board:
Wacebo: open the board's built-in mirroring app.
SMART: open SMART Mirror or iMirror.
i3: open i3ALLSYNC.

Turn on Do Not Disturb before you mirror. Disconnect before you leave so your screen isn't still on the board.

Annotate with the board's built-in tools

No app or account needed. Use these when you want to write on the board right now.

02
No arrows on the edges? Turn on the side toolbar first. It takes a minute and you only do it once per board. See how →
Over anything on screen

Annotation mode

  1. Tap the arrow on the edge of the screen to open the side toolbar.
  2. Tap the pencil. A floating pen menu appears.
  3. Write and highlight over whatever is showing: a website, a video, or your Chromebook.
  4. Erase or change colors as you go.
  5. To save your notes, tap the three dots in the floating menu, tap Save, then choose Google Drive as the destination.
On a blank page

Dabliu Notes whiteboard

  1. Open Dabliu Notes and tap Create new → Note.
  2. Pick a lined, grid or dot background.
  3. The pen, pencil and highlighter are along the bottom.
  4. Tap a tool twice to change its color and thickness.
  5. To save, tap the menu button with four squares in the bottom right and select Save, then choose Google Drive as the destination.

Two sign-ins, and they have to match

On your Chromebook

Annotate.net on the web

Go to annotate.net in Chrome and sign in with Google Single Sign-On using your FPS account, never a separate Annotate password. This is where you build and teach from your notebooks.

Go to annotate.net →
On the board

The Annotate client

Download the Annotate client on the board from Annotate's downloads page, then sign in with the same Google account. The board only appears as a Livestream destination while the client is signed in.

Download the client →
Read this Both sign-ins must be the same FPS Google account. Different accounts is the single most common reason a board doesn't show up in the Livestream list. Sessions also expire, usually overnight, so the board often needs signing back in at the start of the day.

The suggested workflow

Build a lesson once, teach on it live, and it keeps working for you afterward.

A

Before class

once per unit

Create folders and notebooks

One for each class and section, so you can find things later.

↓

Upload your content from Drive

PDFs, Google Docs, Google Slides, whatever you already have in Drive.

↓

Clone the notebook

If the same content is going to more than one class or section, clone it rather than rebuilding it.

B

In class

every lesson

Present / Livestream to the board

Send the notebook to your classroom display.

↓

Annotate from either end

Work on your device while you walk the room, or annotate directly on the board. You can move between the two mid-lesson.

C

After class

pick either, or both

Save as a PDF to Drive

Export the notebook with the annotations baked in and put it back in Google Drive.

Share as a private URL

Create a private link to the notebook and send that instead.

Pro tip: cloning notebooks saves real time if you teach multiple sections from the same materials. Build it once in period 1, clone it for the rest of the day, and each section's annotations stay separate.
